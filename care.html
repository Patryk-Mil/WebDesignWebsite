General Care Information

Everything you need to know about caring for your dog at every stage of life.

Puppies (0-1 Years)

Nutrition

Choosing dog food can be very overwhelming as there are so many different types to choose from. Puppies need a nutrient rich diet to allow them to grow up healthy and strong. You should only buy dog food labelled for puppies. However large puppies should be fed unmoistened dry puppy food by 9-10 weeks; small dogs by 12 or 13 weeks. Puppies benefit from mixed diets that also include some type of protein, fish oils, cooked vegetables and fruits, and unsweetened yogurt to improve gut health.

How Often Should You Feed Your Puppy

Puppies should have four feedings a day, after about 3 to 6 months decreasing it down to three feedings a day. If your puppy still has a potbelly continue feeding them four times a day. After 6 months small dog breeds can make the switch to only two feedings a day; larger dogs take a bit longer. Around month 8-9 small dogs can switch to adult food and bigger dogs at month 12-14.

Note: Do not feed puppies human food as this can make them overweight, reject dog food and experience health risks.

Activities

Puppies need daily exercise and play to burn off energy and settle properly. Short walks and play sessions are great for getting them tired in a healthy way. Since puppies sleep 14-20 hours a day, doing these activities is essential to improve their development and gives owners a well-earned moment of rest too.

Socialising

Socialising for puppies is as important as it is for humans. Puppies can greatly benefit from meeting other dogs and play dates. This will develop how they interact with other animals and even people. Regular socialising sessions a couple times a week can get rid of their shyness and regulate good habits.

Basic Training

Training is an essential part of raising a puppy, as habits they learn early on will shape their behaviour as adults. Basic house training such as where they can urinate or defecate is one of the most important first steps. Trick training can also strengthen your bond and provide mental stimulation.

Vet Visits

Puppies require vaccinations and worm treatments around once a month for the first three months. It is recommended that you watch your puppy and if you see any irregularities see your local vet. You can bring your puppy in for a visit every couple of months for the first year just in case.

If you need any more in-depth information, please contact your local vet or specialist.

Adult Dogs (1+ Years)

Nutrition

Feeding an adult dog is much simpler than feeding a puppy, but choosing the right food still matters. Adult dogs require a balanced diet that supports their energy levels, maintains a healthy weight, and keeps their coat, skin, and digestive system in good condition. Look for dog food labelled "Adult" or "Maintenance", as these formulas contain the correct balance of protein, fats, vitamins, and minerals for fully grown dogs.

Adult dogs benefit from a mixed diet that may include high quality kibble, wet food, lean meats, cooked vegetables, and small amounts of fruit. Foods rich in omega 3 (like salmon or fish oils) help maintain joint health and a shiny coat. Avoid foods high in fillers, artificial colours, or excessive grains.

Important: Never feed dogs chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, cooked bones, or anything high in salt or sugar.

How Often Should You Feed an Adult Dog

Most adult dogs thrive on two meals per day — one in the morning and one in the evening. Feeding guidelines by size:

  • Small breeds — often do best with two smaller meals due to faster metabolisms
  • Medium to large breeds — two meals are standard, though very active dogs may need slightly larger portions
  • Senior dogs — may require adjusted feeding schedules depending on health, weight, and activity level

Activities & Exercise

Adult dogs need consistent daily exercise to stay physically and mentally healthy. The amount depends on breed, size, and energy level:

  • Low-energy breeds — 30-45 minutes of gentle walking per day
  • Medium-energy breeds — 1-1.5 hours of walking or play
  • High-energy breeds — 2+ hours of activity, including running, fetch, agility, or structured play

Socialising

Even as adults, dogs benefit from regular social interaction. Good socialising options include:

  • Dog parks
  • Group walks
  • Playdates
  • Training classes
  • Visits to pet-friendly shops or cafés

Training & Behaviour

Training doesn't stop after puppyhood — adult dogs still need structure and reinforcement. Key areas to focus on:

  1. Reinforcing basic commands — sit, stay, recall, leave it
  2. Leash manners — especially important for strong or energetic dogs
  3. Impulse control — waiting at doors, calm greetings
  4. Mental stimulation — trick training, scent work, puzzle feeders

Vet Visits

Adult dogs should visit the vet at least once a year for a general check-up, vaccinations, and parasite control. You should also bring your dog to the vet if you notice sudden changes in appetite or weight, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, excessive scratching or licking, limping or stiffness, or lethargy or unusual behaviour.

Senior Dogs (7+ Years)

Senior dogs require a gentler, more attentive approach to daily care as their bodies and needs begin to change. While many older dogs remain active and happy well into their later years, they benefit from adjustments in diet, exercise, and routine to keep them comfortable and healthy.

Nutrition

As dogs enter their senior stage, their metabolism slows and their nutritional needs shift. Senior labelled dog foods are designed to support joint health, digestion, and weight control. These formulas often contain fewer calories, higher-quality proteins, and added supplements such as glucosamine, chondroitin, and omega 3 fatty acids.

Some older dogs may struggle with harder kibble, so softer food or mixing wet and dry food can make mealtimes easier. If your dog has dental issues, kidney concerns, or weight changes, your vet may recommend a specialised diet tailored to their condition.

Feeding Routine

Most senior dogs continue to do well on two meals a day, but their portion sizes may need adjusting depending on their activity level and weight. Monitoring their body condition becomes especially important at this stage. Always introduce dietary changes gradually to avoid upsetting their stomach.

Activities & Exercise

Exercise remains essential for senior dogs, but the intensity should be adapted to their abilities. Gentle daily walks, slow-paced play, and low-impact activities help maintain muscle tone and joint flexibility without causing strain. Many older dogs enjoy shorter but more frequent outings rather than long, tiring walks.

Socialising

Senior dogs still benefit from social interaction, though they may prefer calmer environments. Maintaining a predictable routine also helps reduce stress, as senior dogs often rely more heavily on consistency and reassurance.

Training & Behaviour

Training remains valuable throughout a dog's life. Senior dogs may need refreshers on basic commands, especially if hearing or vision begins to decline. Using hand signals, gentle voice cues, or scent markers can help them navigate their environment more confidently.

Vet Visits

Regular veterinary care becomes increasingly important for senior dogs. Instead of yearly check-ups, many vets recommend visits every six months to monitor changes in weight, mobility, dental health, and organ function. Early detection allows for more effective treatment and a better quality of life.

If you ever feel unsure about a symptom or change in your senior dog, contacting your vet or a specialist is the safest choice.
